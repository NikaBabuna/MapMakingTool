# Height

This page owns height. It does not own how crust thickens or thins. That is the [tectonics](tectonics.md) page.

## Height is read from the crust

Height is isostasy. Thick crust floats higher than thin crust, the way a thick block of wood floats higher in water than a thin one. Height is how far the crust stands above the ocean baseline. Ocean crust is thickness 8, and that thickness stands at height 0. Thicker crust stands higher, one step of height for each step of thickness past 8. Thinner crust stands below the line.

| Crust thickness | Height | Where a person finds it |
|-----------------|--------|-------------------------|
| Below 8 | Below 0 | The floor of a trough beside a rift |
| 8 | 0 | Open ocean, the baseline |
| 9 to 15 | 1 to 7 | Ocean crust thickened near a collision |
| 16 | 8 | The lowest continental crust |
| 32 | 24 | The top of an arc or a suture, at the thickness cap |

Height has no memory of its own. It is read afresh from the crust at every step, so it moves wherever the plates carry the crust. How the crust comes to be that thick is on the [tectonics](tectonics.md) page.

## A new world is flat

At the start every cell is that baseline. There are no mountains yet. Mountains appear only after the plates have worked, because the height is a reading of the crust and the crust starts as flat ocean.

## How the map shows height

The Elevation view colours each cell by its height. Below height 0 it paints water, darker the deeper it is. From height 0 upwards it paints land colours, green at the bottom and fading towards pale at the top, and it shades slopes so that ranges stand out. Open ocean stands exactly at height 0, so today the map paints open ocean in the lowest land colour, and only the troughs beside rifts show as water.

The person can look at height, at the plates, or at the boundaries. Changing the view does not change the height.
